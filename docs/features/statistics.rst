.. _features-statistics:

Statistics
==========

The **Statistics** tab shows which cameras and lenses give you keepers,
and which focal lengths you use on each camera. It covers the whole
library and is updated each time you open the tab.

Keepers
-------

A photo is a **keeper** when you picked it, or when auto-pick suggests
keeping it and you have not decided yet
(:ref:`features-library-auto-pick`). Rejected photos are never keepers.
A camera's or lens's **keeper rate** is the share of its photos that are
keepers; the bar splits it into picked (dark) and auto-picked (light)
photos. Hover a bar for both percentages.

Auto-picks need analysis, so rates rise while photos are still being
analyzed.

What the page shows
-------------------

**Highlights**
   The number of photos and rejects, the keeper rate of the whole library,
   and the most successful camera and lens.

**Cameras** and **Lenses**
   One row each, with photos, keeper rate, picked, auto-picked and rejected
   photos, the most successful first. Equal rates put the more used gear
   first. Gear with fewer than 20 photos is listed after the rest, under
   *Fewer than 20 photos*, because a handful of photos says little: a
   camera with one picked photo would otherwise top the list. Such gear is
   never named the most successful.

**Most used focal lengths**
   For each camera that records focal lengths, its five most used ones
   (rounded to whole millimetres), with the share of the camera's photos
   taken at each. The longest bar is the most used focal length.

A camera whose files spell it differently (for example make *Canon* with
model *EOS R5* in RAW files and *Canon EOS R5* in JPEGs) is one row, as
it is one option in the camera filter.

Photos without camera or lens information count in the highlights only.
The camera and lens are read from each photo's EXIF data on import
(:doc:`/features/import`).
